Why students learn: motivation, emotion, and what drives behavior

You have a deadline. You know what you need to do. You sit down, open your laptop, and then spend 40 minutes doing everything except the work. It is not laziness and it is not a lack of ability. Something about the situation, the task, or how you feel about your own competence is making engagement feel impossible. That is a motivation problem, and research has a lot to say about why it happens and what actually helps.

Motivation is not a fixed trait you either have or lack. It is a dynamic psychological state shaped by beliefs, context, and emotional experience. Understanding it changes how you teach, study, and design learning environments.

Deci & Ryan, 1985 Pintrich, 2003 Pekrun, 2006 PISA 2022

Self-determination theory

Three basic psychological needs

Deci and Ryan's self-determination theory (1985) proposes that human motivation is rooted in three universal psychological needs. When these needs are met, intrinsic motivation flourishes. When they are thwarted, motivation becomes controlled or disappears entirely.

Need 1

Autonomy

The need to feel that your actions are self-initiated and aligned with your values. Students who have choice over how they learn show higher intrinsic motivation and deeper engagement than those given no agency.

Need 2

Competence

The need to feel effective and capable in your interactions with the environment. Optimal challenge, where tasks are neither too easy nor overwhelming, is the condition under which competence and motivation grow together.

Need 3

Relatedness

The need to feel connected to others. Even in individualistic academic contexts, students who feel a sense of belonging and care from teachers and peers are significantly more motivated to engage with learning.

Intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation

Not a binary, but a continuum

A common misconception is that motivation is either intrinsic (good) or extrinsic (bad). SDT describes a continuum of regulation, from purely external control to fully internalized motivation. The goal is not to eliminate external motivators but to support internalization over time.

Intrinsic motivation

Engaging in an activity for its own sake because it is inherently interesting, enjoyable, or satisfying. Produces deeper learning, greater creativity, and better long-term retention.

Reading beyond what is assigned because you are curious
Spending extra time on a problem because it is genuinely interesting
Returning to a subject months later for pleasure

Extrinsic motivation

Engaging in an activity to obtain a separable outcome such as a grade, reward, or to avoid punishment. Not inherently harmful, but can undermine intrinsic interest if poorly designed.

Studying the night before an exam to avoid failing
Completing assignments only when they are graded
Participating in class to get participation points

Academic emotions

How emotions shape learning

Reinhard Pekrun's control-value theory (2006) proposed that academic emotions, the emotions students experience in achievement contexts, are among the most powerful and underappreciated influences on learning. Emotions affect attention, memory encoding, strategy use, and self-regulation directly.

⚡

Activating positive emotions

Enjoyment, curiosity, and pride activate engagement and support deep processing. Students experiencing enjoyment in learning use more elaborative strategies and show better retention.

🔒

Deactivating negative emotions

Boredom and hopelessness reduce cognitive engagement and lead to surface processing. Boredom is the most common academic emotion reported by students and is strongly linked to disengagement.

⚠️

Activating negative emotions

Anxiety and shame activate arousal but direct cognitive resources toward threat monitoring rather than learning. Test anxiety specifically impairs working memory during high-stakes performance.

🌍

Cross-cultural patterns

PISA 2022 data across 80 countries shows that anxiety about mathematics is nearly universal, but its relationship to performance varies significantly by cultural context and gender.
