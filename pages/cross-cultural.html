Does self-regulation work the same everywhere?

PISA 2022 surveyed over 690,000 students across 80 countries on motivation, self-efficacy, and learning strategies. The data reveals both universal patterns and striking cultural differences in how students regulate their learning.

PISA 2022 80 countries 690,000 students Comparative research

PISA 2022 data

Self-efficacy across regions

Academic self-efficacy, the belief in your ability to succeed on a specific task, varies dramatically across countries. East Asian education systems consistently show a paradox: high performance alongside surprisingly low self-efficacy. Western systems show the reverse pattern in some contexts.

Regional patterns

Four distinct cultural approaches

East Asia

High performance, low self-report

Students in Japan, South Korea, and Singapore consistently outperform on assessments but report lower enjoyment and self-efficacy than their scores would suggest. Effort attribution over ability attribution is culturally embedded.

Northern Europe

Autonomy-supportive environments

Scandinavian education systems emphasize student autonomy and intrinsic motivation. Students report higher enjoyment of learning and show strong self-regulation, particularly in planning and monitoring behaviors.

Latin America

Relatedness-driven motivation

Students in Brazil, Chile, and Colombia report high relatedness and belonging in school contexts. Social motivation is a stronger predictor of engagement than in individualistic Western contexts.

Southeast Asia

Mixed regulatory profiles

Countries like Indonesia and the Philippines show diverse SRL profiles within populations, reflecting transitional educational systems combining collectivist values with increasingly individualized learning contexts.

Key findings

What the cross-national data shows

01

SRL strategies are universal but their use is not

All cultures produce students who plan, monitor, and reflect. But the frequency, context, and form these strategies take varies significantly by educational system, cultural values, and teacher practice.

02

The self-efficacy paradox

High-performing East Asian students consistently underestimate their own ability relative to their actual performance. This challenges Western assumptions that high self-efficacy is a prerequisite for high achievement.

03

Gender gaps differ by country

The gender gap in mathematics anxiety is present in almost every PISA country, but its size and direction vary. In some countries girls report significantly higher anxiety than boys despite similar performance. In others the gap is minimal.

04

Socioeconomic status moderates SRL

Across all countries, students from higher socioeconomic backgrounds show stronger self-regulation skills. But the strength of this relationship varies: some systems partially buffer SES effects while others amplify them.

PISA 2025 will change this field

For the first time, PISA 2025 includes process-level measures of self-regulated learning across all participating countries, capturing motivation, emotion regulation, and metacognitive monitoring as dynamic processes rather than static traits.
