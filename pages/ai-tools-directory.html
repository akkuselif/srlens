AI tools for learning: what the evidence says

A research-informed directory of AI tools used in education. Rated not by features, but by what the learning science literature says about how each tool affects self-regulation, metacognition, and long-term retention.

Updated 2025 SRL lens Evidence-based ratings

Intelligent tutoring systems

Established AI tutoring platforms

These tools have the longest research track record in educational AI. They use adaptive algorithms to adjust difficulty and provide immediate feedback — the two features most consistently linked to learning gains.

Strong evidence
Khan Academy / Khanmigo

Adaptive tutoring · AI tutor · K–12 & higher ed

Khan Academy's AI tutor Khanmigo is built to ask questions rather than give answers — a deliberate design choice grounded in productive struggle research. The underlying adaptive platform has 30+ years of ITS research behind it.

SRL impact: Supports forethought and self-monitoring. Khanmigo's Socratic prompting style preserves metacognitive demand rather than bypassing it.

Strong evidence
Carnegie Learning MATHia

Intelligent tutoring system · Mathematics · K–12

One of the most rigorously evaluated ITS platforms in existence, with randomized controlled trials showing significant math gains over several years. Uses cognitive modeling to identify specific knowledge gaps.

SRL impact: Strong on performance-phase support. Limited on forethought and self-reflection phases of Zimmerman's model.

Mixed evidence
Duolingo

Adaptive learning · Language · Consumer

Effective for vocabulary and pattern recognition via spaced repetition. The gamification layer drives engagement but can shift motivation from intrinsic interest to streak-maintenance — a known SDT concern.

SRL impact: Weak on planning and monitoring. Strong on retrieval practice mechanics. The streak system can undermine autonomous motivation over time.

Strong evidence
Anki / spaced repetition tools

Spaced repetition · Memory · Self-directed

Not AI in the generative sense, but algorithmically adaptive. Spaced repetition has one of the strongest evidence bases in cognitive science. Requires self-discipline to use well — which is itself an SRL skill.

SRL impact: High metacognitive demand — students must judge their own recall accuracy. This active monitoring is precisely what builds metacognitive skill over time.

Large language models

LLMs as learning tools

The research on LLMs in education is young but growing fast. The central question is not whether they can help — they clearly can — but whether the way most students use them helps or undermines long-term self-regulation.

Use with intention
ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini

General LLM · Multi-purpose · All levels

Highly flexible study tools when used as Socratic partners, concept explainers, or feedback generators. The risk is well-documented: students who use LLMs to generate answers rather than check thinking show weaker metacognitive development.

SRL impact: Depends entirely on how the student uses it. Prompting for explanations and counterarguments builds SRL. Prompting for answers bypasses it.

Promising
Perplexity / AI search

AI search · Research · Higher ed

Useful for scoping unfamiliar topics quickly. The risk is that AI-synthesized summaries reduce the productive cognitive struggle of source evaluation — a key self-regulation skill in academic contexts.

SRL impact: Can support planning when used to scope a topic. Can undermine information literacy and source monitoring if used as a replacement for primary source engagement.

Significant SRL risk
AI writing assistants (general)

Writing AI · Essay generation · All levels

Tools that generate, substantially rewrite, or complete writing tasks remove the cognitive processes that build self-regulation. Writing is one of the primary activities through which students develop planning, monitoring, and revision skills.

SRL impact: High risk of SRL bypass. The planning, monitoring, and revision phases of writing are where metacognitive skill develops — outsourcing them to AI removes that developmental opportunity.

Promising

SRL assessment · Concept explainer · Research-grounded

Designed explicitly around the SRL research literature. The assessment tool asks reflective questions rather than giving instant answers. The concept explainer grounds every response in peer-reviewed theory.

SRL impact: Designed to build metacognitive awareness rather than bypass it. Every response is constrained by what the evidence actually supports.

The question to ask about any AI learning tool

Not: "Does it help students get the right answer?"

But: "Does it preserve or remove the cognitive processes through which self-regulation develops?"

Productive struggle, metacognitive monitoring, and self-reflection are not inefficiencies to be optimized away. They are the mechanism by which learning happens. An AI tool that removes them, however helpfully designed, removes the conditions for long-term skill development.
